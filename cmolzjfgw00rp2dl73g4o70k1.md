In the world of forensic security, the choice between traditional Infrared (Black & White) and modern Full-Color night vision is more than just a preference—it’s a technical decision that determines whether an intruder is identifiable or just a "grey ghost."

## The Physics of Light: Identification vs. Detection

Most standard CCTV cameras switch to B&W because Infrared (IR) light is outside the visible spectrum. While IR provides contrast, it loses "chromatic data." In a legal setting, knowing a suspect wore a "Red Hoodie" is significantly more valuable than a "Dark Hoodie."

## Technical Breakdown: F1.0 Aperture & 0.0005 Lux

Modern high-authority cameras use an **F1.0 aperture**. This allows 4x more light into the sensor than standard cameras. When combined with a warm supplemental light (instead of harsh IR), the camera can maintain a full color profile even in near-total darkness.

| Feature | Traditional IR (B&W) | Next-Gen Full-Color (ColorVu) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Aperture** | F1.6 - F2.0 | F1.0 (Super Aperture) |
| **Sensor** | Standard CMOS | Back-Illuminated High-Sensitivity |
| **Lux Rating** | 0.01 Lux | 0.0005 Lux |
| **Forensic Value** | High Contrast, Low Detail | High Chromatic Detail |

## The Forensic Value of Chromatic Data

When providing evidence to West Yorkshire Police, the quality of your chromatic data can be the deciding factor. ColorVu technology ensures that even in rural areas with minimal street lighting, the sensor can distinguish between subtle shades, providing clear identification of vehicles, clothing, and physical attributes that B&W cameras simply erase.
