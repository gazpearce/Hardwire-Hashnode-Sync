In the field of forensic security, the distinction between 'detection' and 'identification' is the line between a solved case and a cold one. While standard infrared night vision has been the industry benchmark for decades, the advent of full-color chromatic sensors has redefined what constitutes actionable evidence. This guide breaks down the technical physics behind these technologies and why your choice of sensor matters for local security in West Yorkshire.

## The Physics of Light: Identification vs. Detection

Traditional infrared (IR) cameras operate by flooding a scene with light at the 850nm wavelength, which is invisible to the human eye but detectable by CMOS sensors. This results in high-contrast black-and-white imagery. While excellent for detecting *motion*, IR often fails at *identification* because it lacks chromatic data (color). In a forensic context, knowing a suspect wore a "red hoodie" is significantly more valuable than seeing a "dark gray" one.

## The Forensic Value of Chromatic Data

When providing evidence to West Yorkshire Police, the quality of your chromatic data can be the deciding factor. ColorVu technology ensures that even in rural areas with minimal street lighting, the sensor can distinguish between subtle shades, providing clear identification of vehicles, clothing, and physical attributes that B&W cameras simply erase.
